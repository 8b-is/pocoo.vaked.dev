## the read — how a name survives a contract

Yubaba's bathhouse runs on a single cryptographic attack: **strip the provenance**. Sign the ledger, surrender *荻野千尋 (Ogino Chihiro)*, and keep only a single operational token — *千 (Sen)*. Once you forget your true name, you can no longer compute the route home. Even the river spirit Haku has been bound by the same truncation, unable to leave because he remembers only his role and not his river.

The twenty-one tracks trace the exact state machine of recovering that provenance:

1. **The Threshold (Tracks 1–4 · *"One Summer's Day"*, *"A Road to Somewhere"*, *"The Empty Restaurant"*, *"Nighttime Coming"*)**: Hisaishi opens on the suspended fourths and falling piano fifths of *"One Summer's Day"* — nostalgia before the loss has even happened.
2. **The Bathhouse & Labor (Tracks 5–13 · *"The Dragon Boy"*, *"Sootballs"*, *"Procession of the Gods"*, *"It's Hard Work"*, *"The Stink God"*, *"Sen's Courage"*)**: The middle of the album is work — scrubbing floors, tending the boiler room with Kamaji, feeding Lin's soot sprites. In the bathhouse, gold offered by No-Face is counterfeit (`+1*` — plausible, unearned, turning back into mud). What is real is the herbal river-cake, the herbal bath token, and the quiet solidarity of labor (`0`).
3. **The Sixth Station & The True Name (Tracks 14–21 · *"The Bottomless Pit"*, *"The Sixth Station"*, *"The House at Swamp Bottom"*, *"Reprise"*, *"The Return"*, *"Always With Me"*)**: Track 16, *"The Sixth Station"* (*6番目の駅*), is the still center of the entire record — a solo piano and strings moving over flooded rails on a one-way sea train toward Zeniba's cottage. There is no dialogue on that train; only water, sky, and presence. And on the flight back (*"Reprise"* → *"The Return"*), Chihiro remembers that she fell into a shallow river as a child — the **Kohaku River**, now paved over by apartments — and hands Haku back his full true name: **饒速水小白主 (*Nigihayami Kohaku Nushi*)**. His scales shatter in mid-air because **naming is liberation**.

The return lands, Chihiro walks out of the tunnel without looking back, and the twenty-first track — not the orchestral score's own, but 木村弓's solo voice — sings [*Always With Me*](/posts/2026-10-17-always-with-me.html). The arc is not arrival; it is coming back with your name intact.
