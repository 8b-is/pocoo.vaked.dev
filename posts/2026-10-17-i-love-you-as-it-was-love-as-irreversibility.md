## the read — the commit that cannot be rolled back

Listen to what Billie's narrator is begging for in *i love you*:

> "Maybe won't you take it back / Say you were tryna make me laugh / And
> nothing has to change today / You didn't mean to say 'I love you.'"

Why does she ask him to pretend it was a joke? Because she already knows
the physics of the ledger: **some sentences are state transitions, not
messages.** Before the three words are spoken, the room is in state `0`
— deniable, reversible, safe. The instant `"I love you"` crosses the air,
the branch is committed. You cannot un-say it by calling it a laugh.
"Nothing has to change today" is the plea of someone watching the
irreversible commit land in real time.

And *As It Was* is the world on the far side of that commit. It opens
with a child's voice — *"Come on, Harry, we wanna say goodnight to
you!"* — and sprints at 174 BPM into the only honest report from the new
state: **"You know it's not the same as it was."**

Where Billie's track whispers at the threshold, bargaining for rollback,
Harry's track dances inside the irreversibility. The old baseline is
gone; the bell has been rung; the prior state is no longer reachable.
And right in the middle of that changed world stands the four-word
sanctuary that makes the irreversibility worth paying for: **"In this
world, it's just us."**
