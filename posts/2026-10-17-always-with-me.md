## the read — the song after the tunnel

The album moves through Hisaishi's twenty orchestral cues — *"One Summer's Day"*, *"The Dragon Boy"*, *"Sen's Courage"*, *"The Sixth Station"*, *"The Return"* — a score built for scenes inside the spirit bathhouse. The final track steps out of that frame: a different writer, a solo voice and lyre-harp, and a title that speaks of **keeping** (*"always, no matter how many times"*) rather than of a place.

In the film, Chihiro must cross back through the stone tunnel without looking back. The rule of the crossing is strict: if you turn around at the threshold, the return breaks. *Always With Me* begins immediately after that threshold is cleared. Its central stanza carries the entire epistemological thesis of the Studio Ghibli trilogy:

> *"When the mirror has been broken, the scattered shards / reflect new scenery in every piece... / Even if we start from zero again, / let's count the things we already have, instead of the things we lost."*

Twenty tracks compose the spirit world; the twenty-first stays on in the car ride home, with the hair-tie still catching the light. The read is positional and structural — the signal lives in where the song sits: after the ordeal (`0`), after the true name is recovered (`+1`), as the quiet proof that what was real in the crossing stays with you even when the tunnel closes behind you.
