# I like the way you kiss me — the signal that's all amplitude

*artemas · i like the way you kiss me · fine touch from within · vaked.dev*

---

**Artemas — "i like the way you kiss me"** (2024). A dance record built on a
contradiction you can hum: it is a *love song* that refuses to promise
anything. Catchy to the point of coercion, and underneath, honestly ambivalent —
*I like it* and *I don't know if I need it* in the same breath.

If the set here has a spine, this is the third vertebra:

| | the signal | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| Stromae — *Mon amour* | love as **performance** | a polished API over a broken core |
| The Strokes — *Oblivius* | love as **position** | *what side are you standing on* |
| **Artemas — *i like the way you kiss me*** | love as **signal** | **all amplitude, no phase** |

## the wave, read

A wave carries two things: how loud (**amplitude**) and where it's going in time
(**phase**). A promise is a phase — it tells you the shape *next*. This song
gives you amplitude and withholds the phase: *I like the way you kiss me, I can
tell you miss me* — a reading of someone else's wave, asserted as fact, because
decoding it would cost the pleasure.

That's the hook's whole mechanism. It's **pleasure without a contract**: the
contact is real, the meaning is declined. *You're not with me* and *I don't know
if I need it* sit inside the same catchy bar — the ambivalence isn't a flaw in
the song, it's the subject.

## the loop

Like the others, the loop is the point:

- **Mon amour** loops because the delusion is the point — the beat stays in cache.
- **Oblivius** loops until a question becomes a demand.
- **This one** loops because *wanting* is easier than *deciding*. Amplitude you
  can ride forever; phase is where it ends. So it never resolves — it just
  repeats, loud and undecided, which is exactly what the feeling is.

## the honest note

It's not a shallow song; it's a *precise* one. It names the shape of a whole kind
of connection: high signal strength, zero direction. The kiss is real. The miss
may even be real. What's missing is the phase — the part that would say *next* —
and the honest note is simply that some signals are built to be felt and never
resolved. You don't decode it. You dance to it.
