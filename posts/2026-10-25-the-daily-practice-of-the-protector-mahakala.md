a daily protector liturgy, handed down the Kamtsang Karma Kagyu line and written down by successive Karmapas: Mikyö Dorje spoke the Sweet Song that Accomplishes all Aims; Wangchuk Dorje wrote the short assembly prayer; Situ Drogon Rechen added the swift six-armed section; the thirteenth Karmapa gave the pacification of conflict; the fourteenth, Tekchok Dorje, sealed the collection at Tsurphu. the text is not a poem about the protector — it is the practice itself: homage, self-generation, the torma, the invocation, the offering, the confession, the dedication, repeated daily, "recite the invocation mantra while the music is playing".

## the read

the whole liturgy is one continuous offering sequence — the practitioner generates the protector, offers the torma with the eight substances and the sense offerings, invokes with the music, confesses, and dedicates. what holds it together is repetition: every section closes with the same offering formula and the same aspiration, so the daily practice is less a list of prayers than a single folded gesture, reopened at the same seams each day. the protector is invoked as the activity of compassion — "swift acting six-armed wisdom protector" — and the liturgy returns, again and again, to the wish that the teachings remain and that all beings be freed.
