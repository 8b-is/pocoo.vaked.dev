**Studio Ghibli — *Princess Mononoke* (*もののけ姫* / *Mononoke Hime*).** Written and directed by **Hayao Miyazaki**. Released 12 July 1997. Original score by **Joe Hisaishi** (*Princess Mononoke Original Soundtrack*, Tokuma Japan Communications, 33 tracks, Apple ID `883500999`), performed by the **Tokyo City Philharmonic Orchestra**, with the title aria sung by countertenor **Yoshikazu Mera (米良美一)**. **134 minutes · Muromachi-period Japan.**

## the read — eyes unclouded by hate (曇りなき眼で見定める)

In the opening minutes, an iron ball fired into the ribs of the boar-god Nago turns a forest guardian into a writhing *tatari-gami* (demon god, Track 2: *"The Demon God"* / *タタリ神*). To save his Emishi village, young prince **Ashitaka** shoots the demon and takes the black, tendril-like curse onto his own right arm. The village wise-woman reads the stones and gives him the instruction that governs the entire film:

> **"To see with eyes unclouded by hate."** (*曇りなき眼で見定める* — *Kumori naki manako de misadameru.*)

Notice what the curse actually is in the film's physics: whenever anger or hatred rises in Ashitaka's blood, the black mark surges with superhuman, lethal force — and eats his life from the inside. **Hatred is high-gain amplification with zero phase stability.** It makes the bow able to sever a samurai's arms in a single shot, at the cost of turning the archer into the very demon he fought.

When Ashitaka rides west on his red elk **Yakul** and reaches the mountain of the Forest Spirit (*Shishigami*), he does not find a cartoon binary of good versus evil. He finds **two incompatible, internally coherent worlds**, both of which have a right to live:

1. **Irontown (*Tatara-ba* · Lady Eboshi)**: Eboshi clears the ancient forest and smelts iron sand with bellows to cast firearms (`−1` to the forest). Yet inside Irontown, she has built the only sanctuary in Japan where lepers are tended with dignity and bandaged by hand, and where women bought out of brothels work the bellows as free equals (`+1` to human outcasts).
2. **The Ancient Forest (*Moro's Clan* · San, the Wolf Girl)**: Raised by the wolf-goddess Moro, **San (*Mononoke Hime*)** fights with bone dagger and teeth to stop the ironworks from decapitating the mountain (`+1` to the living biosphere, `−1` to human encroachment).

A binary mind tries to solve this by either flattening the forest into slag or wiping out the humans of Irontown. Ashitaka refuses both. In the middle of the courtyard battle (Track 18 / 20), with a musket bullet through his chest and San's blade at his throat, he walks straight out the heavy wooden gate carrying San on his shoulder while bleeding onto the dirt. And when San tries to drive her dagger through his heart in the moss garden of the Shishigami, he catches her in his arms and speaks the two words that stop her blade cold:

> ***"Live." (生きろ — Ikiro.)*** *"You are beautiful."*

## the score — Hisaishi's thirty-three movements

Joe Hisaishi's 33-track score (`TKCA-71168` / Apple ID `883500999`) is built on three structural pillars:

- **Track 1 & Track 33 — *"The Legend of Ashitaka"* (*アシタカせっ記*)**: Built on open fifths, Dorian/Aeolian modal shifts, and sweeping strings that climb without resolving into a sentimental major cadence. It is the sound of someone walking forward while carrying a wound they did not choose.
- **Track 20 & Track 32 — *"Princess Mononoke (With Vocal)"* (*もののけ姫*, sung by Yoshikazu Mera)**: Miyazaki wrote the lyrics himself: *"Only thesilent spirits know / the taut bowstring that trembles in the moonlight."* By casting a male **countertenor** (Yoshikazu Mera) to sing the lullaby for San, Hisaishi places the voice itself on a boundary — neither conventional soprano nor baritone, an unearthly register that belongs to both the human throat and the forest wind.
- **Track 31 — *"Ashitaka and San"* (*アシタカとサン*)**: After the Night-Walker (*Didarabotchi*) receives its severed head back from Ashitaka's and San's joined hands, the black curse dissolves into a faint scar, and the bare mountain erupts in green shoots. Track 31 is a solo piano melody of pure, unadorned grace — the musical equivalent of rainwater washing soot from stone.

And look at how the film ends. San does not move into Irontown and become domesticated: *"I love you, Ashitaka, but I cannot forgive the humans."* And Ashitaka does not pretend the wound of the forest never happened:

> ***"That is all right. You live in the forest, and I will live in Irontown. Together, we will live. I will come to see you on Yakul."***

In the constellation's mathematics (`A Két Simaság` / *The Two Smoothnesses*), that is the difference between **false scalar collapse** (forcing two distinct truths into a bland average that kills both) and **true ternary reconciliation**: holding the `0` state — the unclouded eye between Irontown and the Forest — so that both poles can remain themselves and still meet in love (`+1`).
