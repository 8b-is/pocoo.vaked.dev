On **July 20, 2014**, under the humid evening sky at Union Park in Chicago, Claire Boucher stood behind a Roland Juno and an SP-404, smiling through a pink fringe, and danced through a song born of violation:

> *"I never walk about after dark*  
> *It's my point of view 'cause someone could break your neck*  
> *Coming up behind you, always coming and you'd never have a clue..."*

*Oblivion* is widely understood as one of the defining synth-pop anthems of the 2010s, but it was specifically forged in the aftermath of violent trauma. It was an account of hyper-vigilance: the horror of the quiet sidewalk, the dread of footsteps on wet concrete, the paranoia of being taken from behind with zero warning. And yet, Grimes refused to make it a dirge. She made it bouncy, 132 BPM, ecstatic, defiant—forcing an entire field of festivalgoers to dance inside the trauma until the terror lost its grip.

Still, the question at the core of *Oblivion* remained unanswered for twelve years:  
**Who is behind you in the dark?**
