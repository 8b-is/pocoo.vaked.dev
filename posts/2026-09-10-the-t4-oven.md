# The T4 oven

## a September training diary, part 1 of 2

*the constellation · training-pipeline · fine touch from within · vaked.dev*

---

This is the first of two notes covering September. This one is about the
oven: the training-pipeline that turns a base model into a *lane* — a named,
reproducible adapter — on hardware I do not own. The second note (the
[two-remote trap](2026-09-24-the-two-remote-trap.html)) is about the
infrastructure that carries the results out. Both are the same story told from
two ends: making a thing, then making the thing *survive delivery*.

## the one line

> The value of a bake is not the artifact. It is the **ladder of fixes**
> between the first run and the one that harvests.

I keep coming back to that because it is the honest accounting. A 15 GB
merged checkpoint is nice to have; the set of decisions that let a run finish
on a volatile free GPU is the thing you can reuse tomorrow.

## the oven, as a transport stack

The sprint is not one script. It is a pipeline of stages, each of which can
fail independently, and each of which was hardened by a failure:

- **`sprint_boot`** — the environment bootstrap. Installs the stack, resolves
  versions (this is where `unsloth 2026.9.4` bit — see below), and declares the
  run.
- **pad-declare** — the run declares its shape up front: base, effort, steps.
  A bake that does not declare what it is has no name to harvest under.
- **`peft-scan`** — probes the parameter-efficient surface. Which target
  modules exist on *this* base, not the one the tutorial assumed.
- **text diet** — the corpus trim. What the lane eats determines what the lane
  becomes; a lane trained on the wrong diet is a lane trained on noise.
- **lora-first floor** — train the adapter first, always. The floor is the
  LoRA, not the merge. Everything above it is optional.
- **fire-poll watchdog** — the runner that survives the session. It watches
  the trainer, re-fires on death, harvests on success.

Each of the middle stages is a *narrowing*: fewer base modules, less data,
smaller artifact. The pipeline converges on the smallest thing that can be
called done.

## live visibility

The failure mode of a borrowed GPU is that it disappears without telling you.
So the runner heartbeats trainer state every 15 seconds, and the exec client
is built to survive the bake — it does not hold the training session open, it
*observes* it. `./sprint_watch.sh` tails the stream:

```
step 15/150 · loss 1.9xx · lr 2.0e-4 · eta 00:41:12 · state:training
```

That heartbeat is also the death certificate. When it stops, the watchdog
knows, and the harvest either happens or is recorded as refused.

## what was validated

Two lanes came out of the oven whole:

- **gpt2-xl** ✓ — the reference base. If the ladder cannot train a known-good
  model, the ladder is the bug.
- **qwen3-4b** ✓ — the first "real" lane.

Both of their VM weights died with a recycled session — *pre-harvest*. That is
the point: the weights were never the deliverable. The ladder was.

In flight at the time of writing: **gemma-4-E4B**, whose VLM tower OOM'd until
we taught the run to load `text_only`. Then **Mistral-7B**. Both configured to
auto-harvest, named, into the `PeetPedro/8bis-lane-models` bucket.

## naming — every pick leaves the oven stamped

A lane that cannot be re-identified is not reproducible. Every artifact leaves
as:

```
<base>-lane-e<effort>-s<steps>-<ts>
```

with a `run.json` and a `.sprint.name` beside it. The bucket convention is
`save_to_hf.sh`. The name is the provenance: read it and you know the base, the
training effort, the step count, and the moment — the four things you need to
reconstruct the run.

## the adapter that died on the wire

The gemma lane trained. It was named:
`gemma-4-e4b-it-lane-e75-s150-0911-0900`. And then its **adapter died on the
wire** — the 15 GB merged zip 500'd through the Colab 8080 proxy, the session
proxy wedged, and the free runtime recycled underneath it.

That is the whole essay in one paragraph: *the artifact was made and could not
be delivered.* What we learned is that the delivery step was carrying the wrong
payload all along. Fixes carried into the rebake:

1. **Harvest seats the LoRA adapter only.** The merged model is a convenience;
   the adapter *is* the artifact. 15 GB became a few hundred MB and stopped
   falling off the proxy.
2. **`save_to_gguf`'s output directory is pre-created.** The save path assumed
   a directory that the recycled session had not kept.
3. **The watchdog keeps its marker/refresh posture.** A mid-flight proxy failure
   looks exactly like a dead session unless the heartbeat distinguishes them.

The rebake ran gemma → mistral under the fixed stack.

## the version that bit us: unsloth 2026.9.4

A stack note worth writing down: `unsloth 2026.9.4` **deleted the class-level
save APIs**. Exports now run through `unsloth_save_model` and `save_to_gguf`
at module level. If your harvest calls `model.save_pretrained_merged`, it is
calling a method that no longer exists. Pin the version, or read the source —
the same rule this workspace applies to every dependency.

## the collateral, and the standing wall

Two side-threads landed on the same bench:

- **the V4.1 knob lane** — `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` wrapped for the box. N-gram
  order `N=5` fits the shipped engram tables; `N=6` refuses. Per-token KV
  budget, and an fp8 flake (`v41.sh` / `v41_knobs.py` / `engram_headroom.py`).
- **the blackwell study, absorbed** — Q4_0 KV stays, exact attention stays
  exact, one bulk H2D pass (`docs/blackwell-study-echo.md`). A study that
  *confirms* your current choice is still a study; it earns its keep as a
  recorded "do not re-litigate this".

And one wall, named so it does not get re-discovered: **the M1 HF downloads are
frozen (xet)**. The local sprint is network-blocked. The box is the designed
home; the arrival of an ssh key is the trigger. Until then the oven is Colab.
