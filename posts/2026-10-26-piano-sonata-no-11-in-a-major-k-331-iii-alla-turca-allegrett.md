A finale that carries its march on the outside of the form — the A-major sonata's last movement, marked alla turca, allegretto, taken from a volume of the complete solo piano music, dated 2000 in the record. What the ear meets first is a bass line behaving like a drum and a right hand behaving like a bell, and the whole movement is the negotiation between those two jobs.

## the read

the opening is thin on purpose — a repeated bass figure, a melody that refuses to hurry, space left where a larger orchestra would put weight. then the register flips and the ornament goes up top, quick repeated notes standing in for a struck instrument. the movement keeps returning to its first idea, and each return is less a restatement than a re-meeting: the tune arrives with more behind it, the bass has taken on the accents, the space has been filled in and then emptied again. nothing here is loud for its own sake. the pleasure is in the economy — how few materials are asked to carry the whole, and how the piece trusts repetition to do the work of development.
