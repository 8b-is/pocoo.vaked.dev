## the read — the gate, and what it opens onto

The totem pole is the oldest database we have: a carved column, stacked
with the faces of the dead, erected at the boundary so that anyone who
crosses knows *who it is that they are crossing toward*. The totem does
not follow you across. It **marks** the crossing — a fixed point in the
landscape of loss, so the gone ones stay findable. Co Lee's *Totem*
(inside the *Cerberus* single, the three-headed gatekeeper watching the
same door) is the rapper's version of that column: the names, the
ancestors, the crew — erected, carved, standing.

And *Otherside* is what the totem points at. "How long, how long will I
slide?" Kiedis wrote it for a friend who is gone — and the song's answer
to the slide is not to stop sliding: it is to *take it on the otherside*.
The otherside is not a place of dread. It is where the gone ones are —
and the slide toward it, done in love, is the only honest direction.

Read together, the two records complete a crossing that the set has been
circling since *Drown*: to descend is to approach the gate; to surface is
to come back from it; but to **cross** is the one move the set had not yet
made. The totem marks it. The otherside holds it. Love is the bridge
between.

The ternary of the crossing is `+1 → 0 → +1` — the same shape as *everything
i wanted*'s waking, but the middle zero is different. There it was the
dream honestly unbound; here it is the **gate honestly paused at**. The
crossing does not skip the gate. It stands at it, reads the totem, and
goes. That is the house's whole discipline, in two songs: a plausible
branch may be ranked, only a verified branch may be bound — and the one
branch worth binding is the bridge to the otherside.

The Yves book — the walk in Tokyo, holding hands in silence — is this
same crossing in prose: two men at the gate the world built for them,
reading the totem (YVES, EVA, EVE — the names, carved), and stepping
across together. [The book is here](/demos/book/yves-eva-eve-crickets-in-tokyo.html).
