I. Programmable Material Organization — Why Physical Shape Is an Illusion

Proposition 1 (Non-Identifiability of Function from Geometry): Two manufactured objects O₁, O₂ with identical external boundary geometry G(O₁) = G(O₂) may exhibit opposite functional responses F(S₁) = −F(S₂) whenever their process histories (u₁(t), e₁(t)) ≠ (u₂(t), e₂(t)) freeze distinct internal state fields S = P(u(t), e(t)).

Conventional digital fabrication treats matter as a passive carrier of CAD geometry: a slicer converts surfaces into toolpaths, and quality is judged by dimensional tolerance. In Programmable Material Organization (standardgalactic/laboratory, commit 4c68f3c), Flyxion proves why this account fails across every high-performance material class:

  • Smectic Liquid-Crystalline Elastomers (LCEs): Identical extrusion toolpaths switch actuation strain sign (contraction vs. elongation) depending on nozzle temperature and shear history (T(t), γ̇(t)) that freeze the nematic/smectic director field n(x).
  • Meter-Scale Metallic Single Crystals: Wire-arc deposition with pulsed remelting maintains crystallographic continuity across complex architected geometries despite repeated nucleation hazards—governed by thermal gradient G_T and solidification velocity V_s rather than external contour.
  • Hierarchically Porous Sorbents & Organic Semiconductors: Uptake kinetics and charge mobility depend on multi-scale pore connectivity and domain percolation established during solvent evaporation history.

To make process history scientifically tractable, Flyxion defines Task-Relative Sufficiency for a compressed manufacturing record R = Π(u(t), e(t)): a record projection Π is sufficient for functional observable F at tolerance ε if and only if I(F(S); (u,e) | Π(u,e)) ≤ ε. Anything less discards the causal state that makes the object work.

II. Compression Without Verification — Lock/Fork Geometry & Compressed History

The Continuation-Geometry Thesis (CWV §1–3): Fine-tuning a model on its own unverified outputs (Simple Self-Distillation, SSD) improves pass@1 not by acquiring new certified knowledge, but by reshaping continuation geometry—sharpening high-certainty lock positions while preserving multi-branch fork positions under shifted decoding policies.

When a coding model samples one unverified solution per prompt at temperature T_train and fine-tunes on those raw trajectories (even when truncation removal leaves most samples syntactically incomplete), benchmark accuracy rises. Flyxion refuses the lazy summary ("the model taught itself new reasoning") and isolates the structural mechanism:

  • Lock vs. Fork Redistribution: At lock tokens (where syntax or local invariants admit one valid continuation), self-distillation concentrates probability mass and suppresses tail slip. At fork tokens (where multiple algorithmic strategies branch), diversity survives provided decoding policy mismatch prevents premature mode collapse.
  • Why Compressed History Breaks AI Safety (Admissibility-Lab 3358fb4): When an agent's interaction history or CoT trace is compressed into a summary state h̃_t = C(h_{1:t}) without a formal verifier, admissibility constraints are non-conservative under compression. A policy that appears safe on the compressed summary h̃_t can violate hard safety invariants that depended on discarded provenance—and rolling back model weights after deployment never rolls back external world consequences.

III. Adversaria (02 & 11) — Routing Is Not Adjudication & The Two-Plane Ledger

The Open-Inscription Theorem (Adversaria Ch. 16): For every finite closed set of records with consistent identifiers and acyclic references, the set is a valid ledger under every policy, whatever gates the policy contains. Validity never mentions policy; gates act only on a surface's projection.

In 02: Routing Is Not Adjudication and 11: Inscription and Authority (standardgalactic/adversaria, commit df7cfa7), Flyxion formalizes the exact five-stage pipeline that prevents attention-routing from corrupting epistemic truth:

Stage Plane Input Dependencies Guarantee / Output
1. Inscribe Ledger Storage Record bytes only Open to all actors; appends record, preserves ID, returns cryptographic receipt.
2. Validate Structural Integrity Record + referenced IDs + schema Verifies acyclic references, hash match, schema conformance, and inert payload fields.
3. Admit Authorization Plane Record + Auth Projection + Policy + Ledger Time Assigns 1 of 7 surface dispositions: admitted, duplicate, malformed, unscoped, quarantined, gated, refused.
4. Project Surface View Active Set (admitted records on surface Σ) Communities with different gates maintain distinct projections over the same underlying records without copying.
5. Activate Argument Plane Active Set + Argument References only Computes standing & status labels (IN / OUT / UND). Strictly invariant to routing weights, view counts, reputation, or controversy flags.

Stored, Admitted, and Endorsed are three distinct states. Charging for promotion into shared attention is legitimate; charging for or censoring inscription is a category error. And flagging a claim as controversial may gate its admission to a default surface, but never changes its logical evaluation status (IN, OUT, or UND).
