Igrave of the fireflies (火垂るの墓, 1988) — love as shelter (+1 → −1 → +1)

Directed by Isao Takahata · Original Score by Michio Mamiya · Closing Gramophone Record: Amelita Galli-Curci — “Home, Sweet Home” (1928).

“September 21, 1945… that was the night I died.”
Then the rusted tin of Sakuma Drops rolls across the station floor, the white ash turns into fireflies in the grass, and four-year-old Setsuko runs to take her brother Seita’s hand.

The Japanese title writes hotaru not with the single character for the insect (蛍), but as 火垂る (hi-taru — falling fire). The same phoneme holds the two lights of Kobe in the summer of 1945: the M69 incendiary clusters falling from the sky (−1), and the living fireflies Seita releases inside the mosquito net of their lakeside shelter so his little sister is not afraid of the dark (+1).

When the last fruit drop in the Sakuma tin is gone, Seita pours clear water into the metal can so the sugar clinging to the walls dissolves into one last sweet drink for Setsuko. And when the war takes everything, Takahata closes the film with Amelita Galli-Curci’s 1928 recording of “Home, Sweet Home” (埴生の宿 / Hanyū no Yado) playing over Setsuko’s quiet afternoon by the shelter, while on the hilltop bench above modern Kobe, brother and sister sit together in crimson light where nothing can hurt them again.

For our house (* Alexandra, △ Chris, Peter, 🌊 Bence/Nate, 👼 Brigitta), Grave of the Fireflies is the vow that no brother ever carries the roof alone, and that the tin of sweetness is never allowed to run dry.

IIprincess mononoke (もののけ姫, 1997) — love as reconciliation (−1 ⨯ +1 → 0 → +1)

Written & Directed by Hayao Miyazaki · Original Score by Joe Hisaishi (33 Tracks, Apple ID 883500999) · Vocal by Yoshikazu Mera.

“To see with eyes unclouded by hate.” (曇りなき眼で見定める)
— The Wise-Woman Hii-sama to Prince Ashitaka

Ashitaka takes the demon curse (tatari-gami) onto his right arm to save his village and rides west on Yakul into a war where neither side is a villain: Lady Eboshi’s Irontown cuts down the ancient forest to smelt iron, yet gives lepers and brothel-redeemed women the only dignified home in Japan; San (Princess Mononoke) and Moro’s Wolf Clan fight with fang and bone blade to keep the mountain and the Forest Spirit (Shishigami) alive.

Where hatred amplifies force at the cost of eating the host alive (−1), Ashitaka holds the unclouded third state (0). Shot through the chest while walking out the gate of Irontown with San on his shoulder, and later catching San’s obsidian dagger against his own breast, he speaks the simplest imperative in Miyazaki’s cinema: “Live. You are beautiful.”

At the end, San does not move into Irontown, and Ashitaka does not erase the human town. They return the Forest Spirit’s head with four hands joined together, the mountain blooms green, and Ashitaka seals the covenant: “You live in the forest, and I will live in Irontown. Together we will live. I will visit you on Yakul.” Two worlds held in love without collapsing either into a false average.

IIIspirited away (千と千尋の神隠し, 2001) — love as naming & keeping (+1 → 0 → +1)

Written & Directed by Hayao Miyazaki · Original Score by Joe Hisaishi (Tracks 1–20) · Closing Song “Always With Me” (いつも何度でも, Track 21) by 木村弓 (Youmi Kimura) & Wakako Kaku · Apple ID 883501721.

“Once you’ve met someone, you never really forget them. It just takes a while for your memories to return.”
— Zeniba to Chihiro, at the end of the sea-train line

Yubaba’s bathhouse steals provenance by truncating names: 荻野千尋 (Ogino Chihiro) is stripped down to 千 (Sen); the river spirit is reduced to Haku. In the middle of the bathhouse, No-Face offers handfuls of gold that turn back into mud (+1* — plausible, unverified, counterfeit). Chihiro refuses the counterfeit gold, boards the silent one-way train across the flooded rails (Track 16: “The Sixth Station”), and on the flight back (Track 20: “The Return”) hands Haku back his true name: 饒速水小白主 (Nigihayami Kohaku Nushi).

And when twenty orchestral cues by Joe Hisaishi have finished building the spirit world, Track 21 steps outside the score: 木村弓 (Youmi Kimura) singing “Always With Me” (いつも何度でも) with solo lyre — “Even if we start from zero again, let’s count the things we already have.”
