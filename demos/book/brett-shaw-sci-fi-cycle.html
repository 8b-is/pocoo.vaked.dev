§I Book I — Hogyan legyünk Sherlock Holmes? (How to Be Sherlock Holmes in an Occult Imperium)

In an Imperium ten thousand light-years wide, etiquette is not manners. Etiquette is ballistics. It tells a citizen where to stand so the hierarchy does not have to aim twice, where to lower his eyes when a courtier of the Space Palace passes, and which questions are impolite enough to carry a death sentence.

Brett Shaw had been abandoned on Eklon young enough to learn the first rule of survival before he learned the first rule of grammar: a custom that forbids you from looking at the ceiling is only ever written by someone hiding something on the ceiling.

Fittyet hány sok etikettre, kivéve azokat amelyeknek betartását maga is fontosnak gondolja. (.oOo. Poo .oOo.)

When the local magistrate on the outer ring called the locked-vault murder of a shipping intendant an occult curse — and ordered the doors welded shut out of respect for house protocol — Brett borrowed a dead inspector's long coat, ignored three consecutive decrees of ceremonial silence, and looked up. The burn mark on the vault's upper beam was not a curse. It was the exhaust signature of an imperial clipper-drive regulator smuggled inside a diplomatic reliquary.

∿

Sherlock Holmes, Brett reflected as he pried the reliquary open with a combat knife, only ever had to deduce inside three dimensions and one London fog. Here, in the shadow of the Anstetten dynasty, a detective had to account for four: mass, energy, information, and the parking dimension where the ruling bloodline kept the weight they could not fit inside a drawing room.

— You broke four court protocols entering this room, the magistrate whispered when Brett laid the regulator on the table.

— The dead man kept all four, Brett answered, setting a small amber lamp on the corner of the desk. Look how much good the box did him.

∿

That night, when the Praetorian recruiters came looking for the boy who had solved a diplomatic assassination by refusing to bow at the doorway, Peter Shaw — the man the Guard called Gyémántszív, Diamond-Heart — stood in the rain outside the barracks and looked at Brett for a long time.

— They want to put you in a regiment, Peter Shaw said quietly. The Emperor has another place in mind. First you go to Rosan. Try not to die in the mud before you learn why your blood runs warmer than theirs.

§II Book II — Ópiumkeringő (Opium Waltz — The Alef-Osztag Fire-Pair)

Inside the Alef-osztag, nobody used radio chatter when Brett Shaw and Paul Wittgen took the point. Radio had latency; radio had encryption headers that could be stripped by an astral eavesdropper; radio required turning a thought into words and words back into movement.

Tökéletesen ismerik egymás gondolatait.

On the Kabbalistic stair of Initiation before the beryllium-agate throne, Wittgen had been raised in 2667 to Chokmah — the second Sephirah, the primordial flash of lightning before form — while Brett stood on Tipheret, the sixth Sephirah, the solar heart that holds the tree in balance. When they moved down a corridor together, Wittgen was the strike and Brett was the axis. Before Wittgen's boot touched the deck plate on Roxolán, Brett had already angled his black cloak three degrees left to catch the ricochet that hadn't been fired yet.

∿

Roxolán in 2669 smelled of sweet synthetic lotus smoke, overheated casinos, and infrared chandeliers (Szűz az infrafényben). Before combat drops, standard operatives — even Wittgen, when the old ghosts pressed too hard — took neural and virtual suppressants to iron every tremor of feeling out of the nervous system, turning the face into a stone mask (szobormerev arcú hadi gépezet). Only at the poker tables, when Wittgen lost a hand and left a hundred brawlers groaning in the wreckage of a Roxolán saloon, did the pressure valve blow.

Brett refused the ampoules.

Then came the night of the Pearl of Roxolán (Roxoláni Gyöngyszem). What began as a surgical extraction inside the orbital pleasure-dome spiraled into a crossfire of automated defense grids and collapsing pressure bulkheads. By the time the black hoods of the Alef-osztag stood in the smoking atrium, civilians who had only come to watch the dancers lay beneath the shattered cryolite glass.

∿

Back in the lower-ring bar, seventy percent of Brett's body — the trans-dimensional cybernetic ribs, the Vajra Arm anchored in the transfer plane — hummed without pain. It was the remaining thirty percent that ached until his hands shook on the glass.

Tubs slid a tumbler across the scarred zinc counter and did not offer a toast.

— The medics have a clean wipe for Roxolán, Tubs said. One ampoule and the Pearl is just coordinates in an after-action file.

Brett looked down at his cybernetic knuckles, then at the dark fabric of the hood folded beside his elbow.

Ha kitörlöm, Tubs, akkor már nem én hordom a csuklyát. Akkor a csuklya hord engem.

§III Book III — A Démoncsászár fejvadászai (Bounty Hunters of the Demon Emperor)

Years before the Black Hoods, in the freezing mud of the IMC training camp on Rosan, Private Brett Shaw should have died on an obstacle wire under live plasma fire. He hadn't, because a quiet recruit named Paul Wittgen had walked back into the kill-zone, hauled Brett over his shoulder, and carried him across the line without saying a word.

In 2668, on the methane-snow ridges of Halmer, the debt came due inside the Zero-Sign Zone (zérójelű zóna).

Egy vékony, zérójelű zóna vette körül a testet, mely mind a két típusú töltést vonzza és taszítja egyszerre.

Every csuklyamodul of the Feketecsuklyások was woven from Tartaros mist and linked across light-years to the mind of Emperor IV. von Anstetten. At rest, its thin zero-sign boundary held attraction (+1) and repulsion (-1) in simultaneous Trit 0 equilibrium, shielding the wearer's luminous white soul cocoon (gubó) and spinal column. Its attack sensor had only one blind spot: a completely still or stopped heart.

∿

On Halmer, poisoned by the palace intrigues of Princess Gwandolyn, the remote imperial link severed mid-battle. The Emperor's consciousness departed Brett Shaw's hood without collapsing it.

Instantly, the csuklyamodul inverted into its Trit -1 failure mode — palackba zárt végítélet, doomsday uncorked. Blind, autonomous, and lethal to anything with a beating heart, the hood locked Brett's cybernetic body into a berserk rampage while broadcasting a flare into the astral dark that drew the predator entity Monaterg.

The rest of the squad fell back. Paul Wittgen dropped his rifle in the snow and walked forward alone.

He did not fire on his brother. Stepping straight across the razor edge of the zero-sign perimeter, Wittgen locked his arms around Brett's shoulders to hold him until the hood's charge could spend itself. When Monaterg struck from the astral rift, killing Wittgen and attempting to tear into his corpse to wear it as a vessel, the temperature on the ridge dropped to zero degrees Celsius.

∿

IV. von Anstetten — the Demon Emperor himself — stood on the frozen basalt of Halmer. With a single gesture, he collapsed Brett's berserk hood back into silent cloth, crushed Monaterg's intrusion, and knelt beside the fallen captain. Reaching into the threshold before the silver cord could snap, the Emperor took Paul Wittgen's white soul cocoon and bound it back into his living chest.

When Brett opened his eyes in the snow, Wittgen was breathing beside him.

§IV Book IV — Hosszú az út a trónig, bébi & A Korona hatalma (El-Nath & The 0 °C Crown)

Nobody blows up a star by accident, and the Imperial Admiralty at El-Nath (2665) was not in the mood for philosophical nuances. When the binary core of El-Nath went critical during a covert interception, Brett Shaw had two choices: let the shockfront sterilize three inhabited sectors, or trigger an inward stellar collapse that vaporized an entire imperial battle-group along with the threat.

He pulled the trigger, survived in a shattered escape pod, and was brought back in irons to face a death sentence.

Az energiát fel kellett tölteni tömeggel, a tömeget információval. Az embert sárkánnyal, anélkül, hogy felhasadna.

High above the tribunal, in the beryllium-agate throne room where the air was held at a perpetual zero degrees Celsius to drink ambient magic into stillness, Emperor IV. von Anstetten — wearing his simple dark headband rather than the heavy imperial crown — signed a single line. To the court, it was an inexplicable sovereign pardon. Within the Anstetten-család, it was a father refusing to execute his own illegitimate half-human, half-Megaira dragon son.

∿

The distance between the early Dominate of I. and II. von Anstetten — who had banned esoteric knowledge and ruled through iron terror after 2405 — and the quiet equilibrium of IV. von Anstetten was measured in the Emperor's two daughters.

During the Willis-Moon solar-sail regatta between Earth and Mars, when saboteurs blew the stabilizing rings off the royal yachts, Brett dove through hard vacuum to pull both princesses out of the spinning debris. Gwandolyn, the younger sister, looked at human beings the way the old Dominate did: as fragile, short-lived clay beneath the water-element dragon bloodline.

Caroline-Blindamoor, the older sister, looked at Brett with eyes that remembered their childhood corridors in the Space Palace. Court etiquette forbade any subject from seeing her bronze-red hair unpinned; behind that veil, her true Megaira form was a golden winged giant cobra-dragon (aranyszínű, szárnyas óriáskobra) stored across the dimensional membrane.

— My father wears a headband because a crown makes you forget the weight of the people underneath it, Caroline told Brett quietly in the recovery bay after Willis-Moon. If the throne ever falls to me, bátyám, I will not rule over corpses.

§V Book V — Praetoriánus (“Gyémántszív”) & Minden bolygón jártam már (Planet Rose, 2674)

There was only one human in the Galaxy who could walk up the frost-rimmed steps of the 0 °C throne room and call the Demon Emperor „barátom” — my friend.

His name was Peter Shaw, code-named Gyémántszív (Diamond-Heart), Supreme Commander of the Praetorian Elite Guard and the man who had raised Brett Shaw as his son. Long before Brett ever drew on the black hood, another ancient bond had already been written into the foundation of the Imperium: when the exiled Tyrann graad Baah (I. von Anstetten) first arrived in the Solar System (A száműzött), the Megaira dragons met Pusztai Magor's star-faring Hungarians and sealed an eternal Magyar–Megaira non-aggression and mutual-respect covenant. Even centuries later, any Hungarian in the Imperium could stand before the throne and invoke that Dragon Covenant.

For nineteen years, the 100-strong Feketecsuklyások — unchanged in number since 2552, each initiated one-on-one, each carrying non-standardized gear — had served as the Mahākāla Bernagchen black-cloak shield of the realm.
