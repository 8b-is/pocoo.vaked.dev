I · Alicia Juarrero — Complex Systems, Intentional Action & Causality as Constraint

Alicia Juarrero
Tradition: Complexity theory · Western philosophy
Education: University of Miami (PhD)
Institutions: Prince George's Community College (Emerita) · Georgetown Pellegrino Center · VectorAnalytica
Main interests: Philosophy of action, philosophy of mind, complex systems, causation, neuroethics
Notable works: Dynamics in Action (1999); Context Changes Everything (2023)

Alicia Juarrero is an American philosopher whose research concerns complex systems, philosophy of action, philosophy of mind, causality, and neuroethics. Her work applies concepts from dynamical systems theory, self-organization, and information theory to questions of intentional behavior, agency, and the emergence of higher-level organization.

Juarrero is the author of Dynamics in Action: Intentional Behavior as a Complex System (1999) and Context Changes Everything: How Constraints Create Coherence (2023), both published by MIT Press (DOI: 10.7551/mitpress/14630.001.0001). Her account of causation emphasizes the role of constraints in the organization and persistence of complex systems, with scholarly reviews by Muhammad Ali Khalidi (The Philosophical Review, 2001), Frederick Adams (The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science, 2001), Brandon N. Towl (Philosophical Psychology, 2001), and K. Takaki on Michael Polanyi (Tradition & Discovery).

1. Causality as Constraint (1998) & Dynamical Systems (1999)

Rather than treating an intention as an isolated event that initiates a linear sequence of mechanical forces, Juarrero models intentional behavior as a self-organizing dynamical process. In "Causality as Constraint" (1998), she distinguishes between context-independent constraints (which restrict alternatives without reference to surrounding conditions) and context-sensitive constraints (which establish relational dependencies among components, simultaneously reducing component-level noise and expanding the repertoire of coherent states available to the system as a whole).

2. Enabling, Constitutive & Governing Constraints (2023)

In Context Changes Everything: How Constraints Create Coherence (2023), Juarrero formalizes three tiers of relational constraint:

  • Enabling constraints — generate the phase-space correlations that bring organized wholes into existence;
  • Constitutive constraints — maintain the structural coherence and identity of the emergent whole across time;
  • Governing constraints — regulate the subsequent downward-causal behavior of incorporated components.

III · The Publication Projection: Public Utility Without Public Access (Flyxion, October 2026)

Debates over public data usually treat the choice as one between releasing an observation and withholding it. This monograph argues that the more useful object of analysis is the publication projection — the Markov kernel $P(y \mid x)$ mapping what an instrument observes ($X \in \mathcal{X}$) to what an institution is authorized to release ($Y \in \mathcal{Y}$). Public utility does not require public access to the underlying raw observation; it requires a defensible transformation from observations into outputs sufficient for a specified public purpose.

Constrained Information-Minimization Formulation (Privacy Funnel):
Let $S$ denote the sensitive attributes associated with observation $X$, $T$ the world-state relevant to the public purpose, and $U(P) = -\mathbb{E}[\ell(T, Y)]$ expected task utility pricing missed hazards, false warnings, and late warnings asymmetrically: 
min_P  I(Y ; S)   subject to   U(P) >= u_min
Definition 1 (Four Pairwise-Independent Properties of a Publication Regime):
  1. Output minimization: the released output $Y$ carries minimal mutual information about sensitive attributes $S$ beyond what the specified purpose requires.
  2. Non-retention: source observations $X$ are not persisted beyond the ephemeral window needed to compute the projection $P(y \mid x)$.
  3. Non-acquisition: the physical apparatus is incapable at the hardware/optical level of capturing the identifying information in the first place (the only property independent of operator software compliance).
  4. Verifiable enforcement: an independent outsider can confirm that the deployed device computes the stated projection and persists nothing else.

Worked Spectrum of Cases (Sections 2, 4, 5 & 6)

  • §2 Roadside Hazard Reporting (Connected V2X): Sensor-edge projection emits only short typed hazard records (hazard class, coarse location, timestamp, confidence, expiry) into the public domain while discarding plates, faces, and adjacent property. Analyzes sparse-road density leakage, differencing attacks across repeated releases, and the timeliness-vs-anonymity governance trade-off.
  • §4 Radiographic Quality Assessment: Two pulling objectives — diagnostic quality score vs. anonymizability score (serial-numbered implants, dental/bony biometrics, volumetric cranial scans) — index a routed family of projections.
  • §5 Genomic Statistics as the Limiting Case: The genome is the identifier and implicates unconsenting relatives (Gymrek et al. 2013 surname inference; Homer et al. 2008 pooled mixture detection), marking the boundary where sensitive signal and research utility are entangled.
  • §6 Dual Use and the Question of the Operator: Closes on the institutional question no formalism settles alone — whether an institution can legitimately claim to operate a publication-only instrument while retaining the technical capability to turn that instrument into a surveillance device.
