“I had a dream / I got everything I wanted / Not what you’d think / And if I’m bein’ honest / It might’ve been a nightmare.”
Then the hinge the whole song turns on: “But when I wake up, I see you with me.”

The song is built on the exact distinction the constellation keeps in its ledger: the dream is a plausible branch — everything you wanted, plausibly yours, ranked high, and false. The waking is a verified branch — one person, actually there (Finneas, brother-guardian; in the house’s own graph, Peter & Bence/Nate, Mom * & Dad △). everything i wanted is the doctrine sung: a plausible branch may be ranked; only a verified branch may be bound. Billie ranks the dream — and binds the person.

The music carries the distinction. F♯ minor is a somber key — the dream’s key. 120 BPM is a dancing tempo — the waking’s tempo. Low energy, very danceable: the song moves without exclaiming, the way relief moves. The bass drop that most of the track withholds arrives not at the chorus but at the admission — as if the record itself only commits amplitude to the true sentence: “you say, ‘as long as I’m here, no one can hurt you.’”

And the sharpest line in the set: “Everybody wants something from me / You just wanted to see me.” Every other voice in the dream wants something from her. The verified one wants to see her. That is the whole difference between performance and presence, between a claim and a receipt, between everything and you.
