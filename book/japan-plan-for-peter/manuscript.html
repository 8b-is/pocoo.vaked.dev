iii. The work — the door is already built

Most applicants send a PDF. You send a page that runs: starfield, an interactive lissajous that follows the cursor, scroll reveals — no images, no video, no dependencies, drawn live in the browser. For an interactive-engineering role, the application is the interview question, answered in advance.

Lead with it. Name the role. Say you are equally at home as a Catalyst — the bridge between artists and engineers is the thing your whole body of work demonstrates: lissajoverse (oscilloscope/vector), enthea (real-time visual synthesizer), project-zero (systems in C), pureQTern (C↔Rust, agreeing to the bit).

Tell the truth about the AI. The disclosure is already in the package and it is a strength, not a confession: you stated the scope, and you said who did what. A team that hires for craft will read that as rigour.

Concretely, three moves, in order:

1 — Submit the form. It is the only step that is waiting on you. The package is done; the button is not pressed.

2 — Name the role exactly, from the shortlist, and let them route you otherwise if they prefer. “Open position” is the fallback, not the opener.

3 — Keep the second door warm. Do not apply once. A studio is a season, not a lottery.

iv. The paperwork — the honest part

This is the chapter people skip, and it is where a Japan plan actually lives or dies. Its structure, with the gaps marked as gaps:

statusHungarian / EU citizen — EU work authorization, no sponsorship needed in the EU
gapJapan is not the EU. Working in Tokyo requires a Japanese work visa — a Certificate of Eligibility, employer-sponsored (Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services is the usual category). This is the open item
gaptimeline — visa processing runs on months, not weeks. Name the date you want, then count backwards
gapmoney — a runway figure in yen, and what you will do if the runway ends
gapthe far side — who comes with you, who visits, who you call on Sundays. Chapter v
realthe working languages: English (full professional) and Japanese (studying). Start the Japanese properly now; daily, small, unglamorous

None of this is a reason not to go. It is the reason the plan has a spine instead of a mood.

That is the plan for peace, and it is already written. The instruction in it is unglamorous: sit, recite, return.

vii. Missing you here

He sent a song with the message. Sparkle, from Your Name — the film about two people who keep reaching across a distance that should make it impossible, and who keep finding each other anyway, in the one place distance cannot reach: the record.

That is the shape of this whole plan, and it was never really about Japan. A country is just the distance version. The plan is: go somewhere real, do the work you were built for, keep the practice, and leave a thread to everyone you love that does not fray.

Missing you here → loved you there. The tense changes; the fact does not. Everything else is commute.

So: submit the form. Learn the Japanese. Book the vet. Name the visiting season out loud. Sit with the mantra. And when the capsule light comes on at five forty-five in the morning, do not reach for the phone — for one minute, just be in Japan, and be kind.

That is the plan. It fits on one page, which is how you know it is a plan and not a wish.
