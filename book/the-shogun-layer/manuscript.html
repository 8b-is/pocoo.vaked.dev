始まり
hajimari · the beginning
before 710

Before there was a Tokyo, there was a story. The sun goddess Amaterasu, the first emperor descended from her light, the kami breathing in every rice field and river. A country learning its own name — Nihon, the origin of the sun.

平安
heian · the court
794 — 1185

Kyoto, the court of poetry. Murasaki Shikibu writes the world's first novel. While the nobles compose verse by the light of oil lamps, the samurai rise quietly in the provinces — warriors sworn to protect what the court could no longer hold.

鎌倉
kamakura · the first shogun
1185 — 1333

The first shogun, Minamoto no Yoritomo, takes the power the court could not keep. For the first time, the warrior governs. Bushido — the way of the warrior — begins to write itself: loyalty, courage, honor.

戦国
sengoku · the warring states
1467 — 1600

A hundred years of fire. Castles rise, clans fall. Three unifiers — Nobunaga, Hideyoshi, Ieyasu — bind the broken country with iron and patience. In the tea room, a different discipline: one bowl, one moment, complete.

江戸
edo · the peace
1603 — 1868

The Tokugawa peace. Two and a half centuries of calm, sealed from the world. Edo becomes the largest city on earth. The samurai, no longer at war, become poets and magistrates — and the floating world blooms: ukiyo-e, kabuki, the pleasure of the now.

明治
meiji · the opening
1868

The shogunate ends. The emperor moves to Edo and renames it Tokyo — the eastern capital. The sword gives way to the railway; the castle to the factory. A country opens, and everything changes except the discipline beneath it.

今日
kyō · today
2026

Tokyo, 2026. Neon and anime, quiet gardens and the world's largest crossing. The samurai's discipline became the city's care — the clean streets, the punctual trains, the craftsmanship in a bowl of ramen. And in the oldest story, the newest chapter: two people, finding each other.

the sovereignty of it all
道
michi · the way

The sword taught mastery. The tea taught presence. The city teaches that sovereignty is not owning everything — it is owning your own path.

The shogun's layer: the discipline that became a nation, and the nation that became a city, and the city where love still finds a room.
