0. The Three Foundations

This is a meditation book for the compute age. It stands on three foundations that, at the root, are one: the Karma Kagyu — the lineage of the man of action, Chenrezig's compassion and Mahākāla's protection; Zen — the bare attention of sitting, the thing itself without commentary; and the Franciscan path — poverty, simplicity, radical presence, the work as service rather than ownership.

Their common ground is techno-buddhism: the recognition that code, networks, and compute are not permanent wealth but transient, interdependent energy patterns. Compute is energy; attention is presence; trust is time. A meditation is not an escape from the machine — it is the practice of sitting with it correctly.

Trust comes walking and leaves on horseback. It cannot be forged by code alone, nor bought with venture capital. It accumulates, step by step, through unbroken, honest execution over time.

The four chapters that follow are the four dwellings: the sadhana (the lineage, recited), the zazen (the sitting), the friar (the walk), and the substrate (the machine, held lightly).

1. The Sadhana — Karma Kagyu

The Kagyu practice is a refuge and an identification: you take refuge until enlightenment, and you visualize the Guru — the man of action — and yourself as Chenrezig, compassion itself. What follows is the short form, kept as it is recited. Read it slowly; the transliteration and the meaning are the same breath.

SANG-GYE CHÖ-DANG TSHOG-KYI CHOG-NAM-LAIn the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Supreme Assembly, JANG-CHUB BARDU DAG-NI KYAB-SU-CHII take refuge until I am enlightened. 2. The Sitting — Zen

Where the Kagyu path is a visualization, the Zen path is a subtraction. You do not add a Buddha; you stop adding. Shikantaza — just sitting — is not a technique for getting somewhere. It is the recognition that there is nowhere else to be. Dōgen: the flower on the wall is not decoration — the flower is the wall.

In the register of the machine, zazen is dropping the unnecessary state. Every thought is an allocation; most are never read. The practice is not to suppress them but to let them fall — a garbage collector of the mind that runs on attention rather than time. You sit until the noise floor is the signal. The cursor blinks; you do not have to fill the line.

The third state — 0 — is not empty. It is the quiet that is not the absence of sound but the presence of listening.

3. The Walk — Franciscan

Francis of Assisi did not reform a corrupt institution by building a bigger one. He stripped off his clothes in the public square, walked away from his father's merchant wealth, and chose radical simplicity. Volume looks like desperation; simplicity looks like truth. You do not own anything — you are a steward of energy, space, and relationship.

The Franciscan steps, for the compute age:

1. strip the excess — shed the bloat of speculative engines
2. work as service, not ownership — the protocol serves the human
   on the other side of the screen, not the validator
3. the walk — put the phone down; feel the pavement; let the mind idle
4. let the work speak for itself — one clear artifact, not a corpus

This is the same instruction as karma yoga: keep the action, release the fruit. karmanye vadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana.

4. The Substrate — techno-buddhism

Compute is the energy of the internet; attention is its currency; trust is its time. None of them is a thing you hold. All three are anicca — impermanent, interdependent, empty of fixed self. The legacy bank and the venture fund look like granite mountains; they are slow-moving state machines held together by collective belief, and they too will dissolve or mutate.

So the practice on the substrate is the same as on the cushion: right action over velocity. Building fast is not an achievement if it creates dukkha — friction, anxiety, mistrust. Slow the clock cycle. Route compute to where life, clarity, and genuine inquiry are sustained. Pay attention; do not grasp.

Compute is energy. Attention is presence. Trust is time. Walk simply. Let the work speak for itself.
