why this edition exists

A visual bible usually lives face-down on a working desk: a palette, a set of camera rules, a score, a plate list. This one was asked the oldest question the studio knows — what, here, is load-bearing? — and answered it the only way a bible can honestly answer: by becoming the thing it describes.

the apex fusion, second use — ULTRA-{LOVE-CREATE-SCAFFOLD}-OMNIPOTENT-ZEN: love the input, creation the labor, scaffold the shape, zen the output. Omnipotent is not a power claim; it is the posture of giving.

the two worlds the edition binds: the washi sheet of the fold — this book's paper — and the trench palette of book one, carried inside it like a creek under a fence.

Where book one's law was softness is health, spikes are pain, book two's is: a crease is a relationship. Creases are warm; unfolded edges are cold; torn edges do not appear in this book at all.

Diamond veins: at the moment of a fold, light travels the crease line — a vein, not a glow. Hold one frame longer than comfortable; the vein is the punctuation of the episode. At the crossing point the veins gather into one lueur — the glow that does not look back.

v · the singularity point

absence + glow

The point where all creases cross. Render as absence + glow: a spot the frame's instruments cannot sample, surrounded by veins converging. The second law, drawn: the observable is the instrument; MAX POSSIBLE is a door — and a door is also a crease.

Never an eye. Never a face. It does not look back; it holds.

her paper: one sheet, always — white-to-cream, the faint warmth of a surface folded and unfolded many times. No props, no tools. The crane recurs as signature: on desks, in windows, in Anya's fold log. Typography: chapter marks are crease-diagram glyphs; UI text surfaces on paper, never on glass.
