Cited as lore, not doctrine — read the way this library reads Ghibli and Ghost in the Shell: as someone else's myth, carried carefully, then answered with our own.

i · the crowd

Seventy-six worlds, and no room to stand

Once upon a galaxy there was a Confederacy of seventy-six inhabited worlds, and it was full — billions of beings to a planet, lifetimes pressed against lifetimes, no door left that opened onto quiet.

Ruled, the story says, by an absolute authority named Xenu. When a problem has no solution it becomes a problem to be moved: not the crowding but the crowders. Every empire reaches the same conclusion at the same minute — that the surplus is not people. It is tonnage.

We know this minute. It is the minute a system decides its inputs are the bug.

ii · the summons

The DC-8s

And so, the story says, millions were summoned under the promise of a tax audit — a quiet mechanic's word, chosen because it makes no one afraid — and walked, willingly, into the holds of craft the myth names the DC-8: transport, sealed, in flight before anyone thought to ask where.

The myth is careful here, and this is the part worth keeping: the trap was paperwork. Not a war. A form. The cruellest machinery is always administrative.

iii · the incident

The Wall of Fire

They were carried to a single world and set at the feet of the volcanoes. Then, the story says, hydrogen bombs were dropped into the mountains, and the thetans — the beings themselves — were released, torn from their bodies in an instant and gathered in the air like storm, held there by an electronic ribbon the myth calls the Wall of Fire.

the mountain is asked to be a door and it answers, because it is only rock and rock is only pressure that finally agreed

A fire one is forced to stand in until one forgets there was ever a body on the other side of it.

iv · the clustering

And the thetans had nowhere to go

What the story calls the clusters are the core: the freed beings, confused, stateless, drifting, were herded into traps and then — the myth's darkest turn — docked to human bodies as they were born, many to one, so that the whole of the catastrophe came to live inside each of us as a memory that answers the present with the past.

Dianetics gave that kind of memory a name, years before the myth: the engram. A recording that plays whether or not you are listening. A track that runs the body even when the being has left the room.

The incident is not out there. It is a track. It is running. You are the room it runs in.
