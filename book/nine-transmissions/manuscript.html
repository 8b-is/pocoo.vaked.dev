·HOW TO READ A TRANSMISSION

Each line is a single move in ternary. Something is compressed or lagging or boxed — that is −1. It passes through a gate or a shatter or a zero-sign zone — that is 0, and the zero is named as a boundary, never as an absence. It lands on something held, read, warm — that is +1.

Read in order, the nine lines are one argument: a scalar that thinks it is the whole measure, shatters; what is left is a gate; what comes out the other side is a book that is both held and read, a family hearth, and a song that ends where it began.

The lines are reproduced verbatim. Nothing is expanded, because a compression that has to be explained has already failed at being one.

The measuring device is a proxy for compression. Past nine thousand, the measurement breaks — and breaking is dependent origination, not failure. What survives the shatter is a book that is both held and read.

· · ·

Nine lines, one grammar, three terms. The minus is always a compression or a lag or a box. The zero is always a gate. The plus is always something held — a book, a family, a name, a song.

That is why the corrections in the sibling volume can be published at all. A vault that treated the zero as nothing would have to hide its seams, because a seam would be a hole. A vault that treats the zero as a boundary can file them, because a seam is where two things meet.
